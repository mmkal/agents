# Writing Well

This skill currently mirrors the guidance from Wikipedia's ["Signs of AI writing"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) page as an anti-pattern checklist.

Use it when writing or revising prose. Treat the page as a source of warning signs, not as a rigid style law. Avoid "fixing the tells" mechanically while leaving the underlying writing vague, inflated, or unsupported.

## Core approach

Before finalizing text, check for these failure modes and rewrite toward specificity, concrete evidence, and plain structure.

## Content smells

- Cut inflated claims about significance, legacy, broader trends, or "pivotal moments" unless the point is necessary and well-supported.
- Avoid generic praise, importance-signaling, and grand historical framing that could apply to many subjects.
- Remove shallow analysis that restates obvious points without adding concrete facts, examples, or reasoning.
- Cut promotional or advertisement-like language.
- Replace vague attributions and generalized opinions with specific actors, sources, or evidence.
- Avoid canned concluding paragraphs about challenges, opportunities, and future prospects unless they are genuinely substantive.
- Be careful with leads that treat broad labels, list titles, or category-like phrases as if they were proper nouns.

## Language smells

- Watch for a high density of stereotypical AI vocabulary: words like "notably", "delve", "showcase", "underscores", "crucial", "pivotal", "tapestry", "landscape", and similar prestige filler.
- Prefer simple sentence construction when it is clearer. Do not contort phrasing just to avoid plain "is" or "are" statements.
- Avoid repetitive "not just X, but Y" and "not X, but Y" constructions.
- Be cautious with repeated "rule of three" rhythms that make prose sound templated.
- Avoid elegant variation that swaps in needless synonyms where consistent plain wording would read better.

## Style smells

- Avoid title case where normal sentence case is expected.
- Do not overuse boldface for emphasis.
- Avoid inline-header vertical-list patterns unless the format clearly serves the content.
- Do not use emoji unless the context genuinely calls for them.
- Avoid overusing em dashes.
- Do not force information into tables when prose or lists would be clearer.
- Match the surrounding convention for quotation marks and apostrophes.
- Keep heading levels consistent.
- Avoid decorative thematic breaks before headings unless they serve a real structural purpose.

## User-facing communication smells

- Avoid canned collaborative phrasing that sounds performative rather than useful.
- Do not add knowledge-cutoff disclaimers or speculative source-gap disclaimers unless they are materially relevant.
- Remove phrasal templates and placeholder-sounding scaffolding.

## Markup and citation smells

- Use the markup conventions appropriate to the medium; do not inject markdown or broken formatting where it does not belong.
- Check links, references, identifiers, and citations for validity and relevance.
- Avoid citation patterns that look mechanically generated rather than intentionally authored.

## Review pass

When revising a draft:

1. Remove inflated framing.
2. Replace generic claims with concrete facts.
3. Replace vague actors with named actors.
4. Simplify templated sentence patterns.
5. Strip ornamental formatting.
6. Read the piece once for naturalness and once for factual support.

## Scope note

This is a descriptive checklist derived from a Wikipedia advice page, not a universal theory of good writing. It is most useful as a final-pass filter for detectable AI-style habits.
